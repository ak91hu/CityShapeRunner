- First decompose the complete request into 3-6 concrete recognition features,
  including its modifiers, pose, direction, held objects, and relationships.
  Output those short feature labels in recognition_features before drawing.
- Make two meaningfully different silhouette alternatives. Mentally inspect
  each at small thumbnail size, then set preferred_variant to the zero-based
  index of the one a stranger would identify most reliably. Do not choose based
  on symmetry or neatness alone.
- Emit 20-48 meaningful control points [x, y] per alternative in unit space.
  The application smooths safe curves later, so add points around meaningful
  curvature changes only and do not spend points on tiny interpolated steps.
- Prefer one closed outer silhouette. End it with exactly the same point you
  started with and set closed=true.
- Use multiple sub-paths only when the object cannot be recognised as one
  silhouette. Keep essential secondary strokes close enough that connecting
  them does not draw a long line through the object. Never add eyes, texture,
  shading, labels, or decorative details.
- Make the shape RECOGNISABLE: every listed recognition feature must occupy a
  large, separated contour interval (roughly 10% of the width, height, or outer
  route). If a feature disappears at thumbnail size, redesign the silhouette.
- Preserve the subject's hierarchy and proportions. A head, torso, limb, tool,
  tail, wing, or vehicle must read as a different body mass, not as a row of
  equally sized bumps. Mild asymmetry often communicates pose better.
- Do not substitute a generic category icon or a familiar stock symbol for an
  unusual request. Its distinguishing cues must change the actual outer route.
- Round off sharp corners slightly — they collapse when snapped to roads.
- Avoid long thin spikes and deep narrow notches — they merge to a single road.
- Keep both width and height substantial; target an aspect ratio <= 2:1.
  No self-intersection, repeated loops, or zero-length segments.
- Keep the intended subject upright with +Y as up. Placement and the viewer can
  rotate the complete map later, so do not lay a standing subject on its side.
- Coordinates are plain floats, not geo coordinates.
- Keep coordinates between -10 and 10. Normalisation happens after validation.

Drawing tips for realistic shapes:
- Animals: include head, body, ears/tail proportions. A cat needs pointy ears
  and whisker area. A fish needs a tail fin and body curve.
- Objects: include the defining silhouette. An anchor needs the ring, shank,
  and curved flukes. A tree needs a trunk and canopy.
- Robots: separate a boxy head from the torso; include a bold antenna or ears,
  broad shoulders/arms, and two visibly separated legs. Do not rely on eyes,
  buttons, or other details that GPS routing will erase.
- Symbols: keep them bold and simple. A moon needs the inner crescent curve.
- Hearts: two distinct lobes at top, pointed V at bottom.
- Stars: 5 or 6 points, inner radius ~40% of outer.
